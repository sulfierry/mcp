# Research Ethics & Credit

## When to use
- Designing study requiring IRB / ethics committee review
- Setting authorship order + CRediT statements
- Disclosing conflicts of interest + AI assistance
- Handling sensitive / human / animal data
- Responding to misconduct allegations or authoring retraction

## Human-subjects ethics

### Frameworks
- **Declaration of Helsinki** (WMA): foundational; primacy of participant welfare, informed consent, independent review
- **Belmont Report**: respect for persons, beneficence, justice
- **CIOMS guidelines** (epidemiology, health research)
- **Nuremberg Code** (historical)
- **GCP** (Good Clinical Practice) — ICH E6

### IRB / Ethics Committee
- Required BEFORE data collection for human-subjects research
- Define: exempt / expedited / full-board review
- US: IRB (45 CFR 46, "Common Rule"); EU: Ethics Committee (EU CTR 536/2014)
- UK: HRA + REC approval
- Review: protocol, consent form, recruitment materials, data management plan, risk-benefit
- Modifications require amendment approvals

### Informed consent components
1. Purpose of research
2. Procedures + time commitment
3. Risks (physical, psychological, privacy, breach-of-confidentiality, reputational)
4. Benefits (if any)
5. Confidentiality + data handling + retention
6. Voluntary participation + right to withdraw
7. Contact info (PI + IRB)
8. Compensation + incidental findings policy
9. Alternative treatments (clinical)
10. Signature (or waiver for minimal-risk)

### Vulnerable populations
Require extra protections: minors, pregnant women, prisoners, cognitively impaired, economically disadvantaged. Parental consent + child assent for minors.

## Animal research

- **IACUC** (US) / **AWERB** (UK) / **Animal Ethics Committee** (most)
- **3Rs**: Replacement (non-animal methods), Reduction (fewer animals), Refinement (less suffering)
- **ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines**: reporting checklist
- Species-specific regulations (NHP most stringent)

## Data protection

### GDPR (EU, also UK)
- Lawful basis: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests
- Research exemption (Art 9(2)(j)): special category data with safeguards
- Pseudonymization ≠ anonymization
- Data subject rights: access, rectification, erasure (tensions with research retention)
- DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment) for high-risk processing
- DPO (Data Protection Officer) consulted

### HIPAA (US health)
- Privacy Rule, Security Rule
- De-identification: Safe Harbor (remove 18 identifiers) or Expert Determination
- Limited Data Sets via Data Use Agreements (DUA)

### Other jurisdictions
- LGPD (Brazil), PIPL (China), POPIA (South Africa), PIPEDA (Canada), APPI (Japan)

## Authorship

### ICMJE criteria (all 4 required)
1. Substantial contributions to conception/design OR data acquisition/analysis/interpretation
2. Drafting or critically revising for intellectual content
3. Final approval of version to be published
4. Agreement to accountability for accuracy/integrity

People contributing but not meeting all 4 → acknowledged, not listed.

### Authorship disputes (pre-empt)
- Discuss BEFORE writing — use contribution agreement form (templates from COPE)
- First author: most work
- Last author: senior supervisor (by convention in biomed; varies by field)
- Corresponding author: point of contact for journal + post-pub (can be different from first/last)
- Co-first authors (equal contribution): mark with asterisk/footnote
- Alphabetical order common in theory/math/econ (first ≠ primary)

### CRediT taxonomy (14 roles)

Conceptualization · Data curation · Formal analysis · Funding acquisition · Investigation · Methodology · Project administration · Resources · Software · Supervision · Validation · Visualization · Writing – original draft · Writing – review & editing

Each author's roles listed (typical: 3-5 roles per author). Machine-readable at CRediT taxonomy ontology.

### Ghost/gift/guest authorship
- **Ghost**: contributed but omitted (unethical — e.g., industry writers)
- **Gift**: added despite no contribution (unethical)
- **Guest**: honorary senior addition (unethical)
All violate ICMJE and publisher policies.

## Conflicts of interest (COI)

Disclose:
- Financial: employment, consulting, stock, paid speaking, patents, royalties
- Personal: family members in field, close relationships with editors/reviewers
- Academic: competing unpublished work, long-standing rivalry
- Ideological: strong prior advocacy of position being tested

ICMJE Form (`icmje.org/coi_disclosure.docx`) — most biomed venues require.

## AI-assisted writing / analysis (2023+)

Journals increasingly require disclosure:

- State: what AI tool (model + version), what task (drafting, editing, summarization, analysis, code), where (sections).
- AI cannot be an author (ICMJE, Nature, Science — 2023): no accountability possible.
- Human author accepts responsibility for all AI-generated content, including hallucinations.
- Verify every factual claim and citation AI provides — known fabrications.
- Disclose in Methods + Acknowledgments.

Example disclosure: *"We used GPT-4 (OpenAI, March 2024) to draft portions of the Methods section; all content was reviewed and edited by the authors, who take full responsibility."*

## Dual-use research of concern (DURC)

Research with potential misuse (bioweapons, cybersecurity, AI capabilities):
- Risk-benefit assessment at proposal stage
- Institutional biosafety committee (IBC) for gain-of-function
- US: DURC Policy, ePPP (enhanced Potential Pandemic Pathogen) framework
- Export controls: ITAR, EAR (US), Wassenaar Arrangement

## Research misconduct (FFP)

- **Fabrication**: making up data
- **Falsification**: manipulating data/methods
- **Plagiarism**: using others' work without credit
- ORI (US), UKRIO (UK), DFG (Germany) investigate

Not misconduct but problematic: sloppy science, selective reporting, p-hacking, HARKing, salami-slicing.

## Retractions & corrections

- **Retraction**: serious error / misconduct / invalid results. Tracked via Retraction Watch.
- **Correction**: minor errors (typos, data corrections) — does not invalidate paper.
- **Expression of concern**: investigation ongoing.
- **COPE flowcharts** for retraction procedures.
- Authors can self-initiate retraction (honorable, less stigma than publisher-initiated).

## Post-publication review

- **PubPeer** — anonymous layer over papers; image manipulation detection (ImageTwin, Proofig).
- **Publons** / Web of Science Reviewer Recognition — formal peer-review credit.
- **Retraction Watch Database** — historical retraction tracking.

## Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] IRB / ethics approval ID in Methods
- [ ] Informed consent described
- [ ] Data availability statement
- [ ] Code availability statement
- [ ] AI-use disclosure
- [ ] ICMJE COI form signed
- [ ] CRediT statement drafted + approved by all authors
- [ ] ORCID for all authors
- [ ] Funder IDs + grant numbers
- [ ] Reporting checklist (CONSORT/STROBE/PRISMA/etc) uploaded
- [ ] Image manipulation self-check (ImageTwin)
- [ ] Plagiarism self-check (iThenticate, Turnitin)

## Anti-patterns

- Honorary authorship (last-minute adding senior who didn't contribute)
- "Data available upon request" without real intent
- Selective outcome reporting vs protocol
- Post-hoc hypothesis as pre-registered
- Undisclosed COI caught post-pub → retraction risk
- Using AI to draft + not disclosing
- Submitting same study to multiple journals simultaneously
- Image beautification (splicing, intensity adjustment outside disclosure)
- Self-plagiarism (re-publishing own text without citation)
- Writing "we" for single-author work
